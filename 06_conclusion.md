# Conclusion

This project produced a dynamic H3/day riskscape framework for the Falkland Islands region, linking environmental feature generation, telemetry-derived species-use modeling, fishing exposure, environmental plausibility, and relative risk mapping in a reproducible workflow. The resulting products separate three related layers: predicted species use, realized risk where predicted use overlaps observed fishing activity, and latent risk where potential interaction risk is mapped independently of observed fishing activity.

The final workflow selected an Extra Trees species-use model under SOM-hierarchical k=30 grouped environmental validation, with a Bayesian/Gaussian mixture component retained as an environmental plausibility layer. This separation is important: the Extra Trees model provides the primary species-use surface, the plausibility layer identifies predictions made under weaker environmental support, and the risk products translate these components into realized, latent, and operator-facing planning summaries.

The seascape analysis provided more than a visual classification. SOM-hierarchical classes supported environmental validation, helped describe which environmental regimes were represented by the telemetry observations, and provided an exploratory test of whether broad seascape classes could summarize species-use and risk structure. The seascape-conditioned surrogate retained broad seasonal and spatial patterns, but the primary H3 prediction surfaces remain the appropriate basis for identifying localized high-risk areas because they preserve finer spatial gradients.

The current products should be interpreted as relative planning and interpretation layers rather than calibrated bycatch probabilities. These results identify several clear paths forward: expand the biological observation base, add independent observer bycatch data for calibration and evaluation, strengthen the role of seascapes as validation and interpretation layers, refine gear-specific risk interpretation, and develop forecasting extensions that use recent and predicted environmental and fishing conditions. The present framework establishes the shared spatial, temporal, and computational structure needed to turn heterogeneous environmental, telemetry, and fishing data into repeatable bycatch-risk products.
