# Conclusion

This project produced a dynamic H3/day riskscape framework for the Falkland Islands region, linking environmental feature generation, telemetry-derived species-use modeling, fishing exposure, environmental plausibility, and relative risk mapping. The resulting products distinguish predicted species-use structure from realized exposure, allowing high-use areas to be interpreted separately from areas where fishing activity creates potential interaction risk.

The seascape analysis provided a useful final comparison. Feature-only KMeans classes summarized recurring environmental regimes, but they did not preserve the localized hotspots captured by the full hybrid species-use model. This confirms that seascapes are valuable for interpretation and environmental context, while the full Extra Trees/Bayesian-Gaussian mixture species-use surfaces remain the appropriate basis for hotspot detection and risk estimation.
